Across the fretboard

Start with the sound, then find a playable route across the neck.

Explore notes and shapes

Choose a root, scale or mode in Explore. The guitar and piano views show the same musical material. Set tuning, capo and handedness before working with positions.

Chord lab generates voicings from chord tones and tuning. It ranks compact shapes and familiar open/CAGED forms. There is no scraped chord-voicing database. Suggestions are starting points; your hand and musical context decide what is comfortable.

Triads across the neck

Use an original example or a suitable library progression. Choose a string set, see inversions, follow small movements between chords, then make a short response.

Microphone activities in this pathway listen for individual pitches. A guitar chord is not reliably assessed as six independent notes through this input.

Jazz guide tones

In Reharmonise and improvise, play the third and seventh through the chord changes. Hear a shape, imagine its resolution, and compare the bass under the same upper voices.

The guitar playing step is recorded as your own report. The improvisation recorder currently accepts piano/MIDI, not guitar microphone audio. A reflection on a guitar phrase is useful practice evidence, but it is not treated as a measured performance score.

Tuner and microphone

Open Practice tools → Tuner and allow microphone access when macOS asks. Use one clear note, reduce background sound and avoid letting app playback feed the microphone. Audio is analysed on the Mac.

Pitch detection can help with tuning, sung intervals and single-note ear-to-fretboard work. It does not establish fingering, string choice, tone quality or polyphonic accuracy.

Find your picking pattern

In Learn, choose a reviewed library passage and follow a trail from hearing and tapping to muted strings, one chord and the changes. Compare Travis picking, a classical arpeggio and strumming with linked notation and tab. The first version supports one to eight bars of 4/4 with chord changes at bar lines.

Hear the bass or upper strings alone, practise a two-bar join, then return to the whole phrase. At the creative finish, choose a pattern and accents and save a separate accompaniment in Studio. Its source piece lists it under My accompaniments; reopening it keeps your edits. Later, try an independent return before revealing the pattern.

The optional one-note microphone check stores pitch windows and signal-quality observations locally. You can inspect the trace and choose to discuss a summary with the coach. It does not grade the full pattern, exact timing, fingers or stroke direction. Tapping and technique reports remain clearly labelled self-assessment. Let the phrase speak

In Learn → Practice & review → Microphone coaching, choose a reviewed single-note guitar voice from your library, or try the original four-bar example. Select one to four complete bars. The score and tab stay together, and Open full piece returns to the music around them.

Choose the Mac mic or USB interface in System Settings → Sound. In Cadence, choose the input channel carrying your guitar. To assess timing, use Measure delay: stay silent while the Mac mic hears eight tones from your speakers, or use an explicit internal/physical loopback with your interface. Keep the same channel and audio routing after measuring. If a direct input cannot hear the tones, select Pitch only; timing stays unassessed.

Choose Count in & listen. Cadence leaves the final count-in beat silent so the microphone can settle. Setup collapses while you follow the moving cursor; Show setup brings it back. You can also choose Focus on the score before a pass. Review pitch and entrance feedback after the pass. Ambiguous, clipped, interrupted or contaminated input is inconclusive. You can mark an uncertain match before keeping the take, retry a slower bar, reconnect the surrounding phrase and return to the whole phrase.

Saved takes retain the source revision and measured evidence. After a complete baseline, an independent return becomes available at least 20 hours after practice or hearing the guide. Guided, discarded and interrupted practice stays separate from independent recall. If the source changes, an older take can be kept as historical evidence without credit against the new score.

Audio samples stay in temporary memory and are not saved or uploaded. Discussing a kept take with the coach sends its score context and a compact per-note summary only when you send a question. This first version checks clean single notes at 50–140 bpm, with at least 220 ms between attacks. It does not grade chords, bends, note lengths, dynamics, fingering or stroke direction. Real-device validation is still needed; the 0.36.1 download predates this work.
